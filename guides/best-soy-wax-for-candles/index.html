Soy wax is what most people start with when they make candles at home, and for good reason. It melts at a low temperature, it’s easy to clean up with hot soapy water, and it sticks to the inside of a cup or jar. But “soy wax” covers several quite different waxes, and buying the wrong one is one of the commonest reasons a first candle goes wrong.

How we chose

We haven’t poured every soy wax on the UK market, and suppliers change their ranges from time to time, so we’ve picked by type rather than by brand. We judged each kind by how well it works in a teacup or other container: how it sticks to the sides, how smooth the top sets, how much fragrance it holds and how forgiving it is for a beginner. The links take you to current listings so you can compare suppliers.

Container soy or pillar soy

This is the one thing to get right. Container soy is soft and made to stay stuck to the inside of whatever you pour it into. Pillar soy is harder and made to shrink a little as it sets, so a candle can be turned out of a mould and stand on its own. Pour pillar soy into a teacup and it pulls away from the sides, leaving gaps and an uneven burn.

The packet or listing should say which it is. If it doesn’t, ask, or choose another seller. Well-known container soy waxes sold in the UK include Golden Wax 464, NatureWax C-3 and EcoSoya CB-Advanced, though any container soy from a reputable candle supplier will do.

Paraffin-soy blends. These give brighter colours and stronger scent than soy on its own, with less frosting. Some people prefer an all-plant wax, but there’s nothing wrong with a blend if it suits you.

Frosting and other things that are normal

Soy often develops a white, crystal-like bloom on the top or down the sides after a while. This is called frosting. It’s cosmetic and doesn’t affect how the candle burns. Pouring a little cooler and warming the cup first both help, and blends frost less. Soy can also set with a slightly rough top on the first pour. A quick pass with a heat gun or hairdryer smooths it out.

How much to buy

A typical teacup takes somewhere between 150g and 250g of wax, so a 1kg bag makes four to six candles. Our how much wax calculator works it out for your own cups. For a first go, 1kg is plenty. Buy more once you know you like the wax.

Storing it

Keep soy wax in a sealed bag or tub somewhere cool, dry and out of direct sun. It keeps well for a long time, though very old wax can pick up smells and dust, so it’s best used within a year or two.
