A hamper feels generous in a way a single present doesn’t. There’s the fun of unpacking it, and you can build it around what the person likes. A candle sits at the heart of a lot of gift hampers, and a teacup candle suits one particularly well, because it goes with the tea, biscuits and treats that hampers tend to hold. This guide covers buying a ready-made candle hamper and making your own.

Buying a ready-made hamper

Candle hampers range from a single candle in a box with a few extras to big wicker baskets. The better ones tell you exactly what’s inside, including what the candle is made of, what it smells of and how big it is. Be wary of listings that only show a photo of a full basket, as the candle can turn out to be a small tealight tucked in among the straw.

What to check.

  • The candle itself. The wax, the scent and the size, or at least the weight.
  • What else is in it, item by item, so you can tell whether it’s worth the price.
  • How it’s packed for the post. Glass, china and food all need proper packing.
  • Best before dates on any food, if you’re buying well ahead of Christmas.

eBay has a wide range of candle hampers from small makers and gift shops, which is handy for comparing what’s in each one.

Making your own teacup candle hamper

Making your own costs less than you might think, and it means everything in it was chosen for the person. Start with the container, then the candle, then the extras.

The container. A small wicker basket, a wooden crate or a sturdy gift box all work. Line it with shredded paper or tissue so things don’t rattle about. If it’s being posted, a strong box is safer than a basket.

The candle. A teacup candle with its saucer is the centrepiece. You can buy one from a maker, or pour one yourself with our teacup candle instructable. Our round-up of the best teacup candle gifts names a few UK makers.

The extras. These are where you make it personal. A few that go well with a teacup candle:

  • Loose-leaf tea or good tea bags, ideally a blend they don’t usually buy.
  • Shortbread, biscuits or a small jar of jam.
  • A wick trimmer or snuffer, which makes every candle burn better.
  • A box of long matches.
  • A pretty tea towel or a small vintage plate.
  • A handwritten care card for the candle.

Three themes that work

Afternoon tea. A floral teacup candle, a packet of loose-leaf tea, shortbread, a little jar of jam and a vintage cake plate. It suits mums, grandmas and anyone who loves a proper cup of tea.

A quiet night in. A teacup candle in a calming scent such as lavender or vanilla, a wax melt or two, a nice hand cream, hot chocolate and a good paperback. Our round-up of the best wax melt warmers helps if you’d like to add a burner.

Packing a hamper safely

  • Keep scented candles away from food. A scented candle in a closed hamper can make biscuits, chocolate and tea pick up its smell. Wrap the candle in cellophane or a sealed bag, put it in its own corner, or choose an unscented one if the hamper is mostly food.
  • Pad the china. Wrap the cup and saucer separately in bubble wrap or tissue, and pack shredded paper around them so nothing knocks against them.
  • Keep it cool. Soy wax and chocolate both soften in warmth, so don’t leave a finished hamper near a radiator or in a hot car.
  • Add the care card. Trim the wick to 5mm before lighting, burn for two to three hours the first time, never for more than four hours at once, and stop when there’s about 1cm of wax left. Our safety and care guide has the full list.
