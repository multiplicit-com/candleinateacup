A candle in a teacup suits nearly everyone. It looks pretty on a shelf, it’s useful on a winter evening, and the cup is still there to keep once the wax has gone. These are the teacup candles we’d actually give, from real UK makers, with each link going straight to the candle or collection we describe.

How we chose

We looked for UK makers whose listings tell you properly what you’re getting: the maker and pattern of the cup, the wax, the scent and how long it takes to arrive. We checked every link on 23 November 2026. We haven’t bought from these makers ourselves, so what follows is what their listings say, not a review of how the candles burn. Handmade listings sell out and change, so if one has gone, the maker usually has others like it.

Our top pick: The Lime Garden’s Duchess teacup candle

The Lime Garden’s Duchess Vintage Tea Cup Candle on Folksy is the kind of present we’d be pleased to give. The cup and saucer are Duchess bone china in the Linden Lea pattern, about forty years old. Duchess was one of the smaller Staffordshire firms, and their floral patterns suit a candle very well.

The wax is 100% EcoSoya, a container soy left in its natural ivory colour. You choose the scent when you order: grapefruit and rose is suggested, and sweet pea, rose, jasmine, vanilla, lavender and apple are the other options. It’s poured to order, so allow about five days before it’s posted.

Why it’s our pick. The listing tells you the maker, the pattern, the age, the wax and the scent choices, which is more than most sellers do. If you’re buying for someone who knows their china, that matters. Our profile of The Lime Garden has more, and our guide to Staffordshire china explains why cups like this are so easy to love.

For more choice: The Vintage Hamper Company

If you’d like to compare several cups side by side, The Vintage Hamper Company’s vintage china candle collection had 17 scented soy candles in vintage fine bone china when we looked. It’s set up as a gift shop, so it suits someone who wants a candle ready to give rather than made to order.

The collection page doesn’t list the china makers or the scents, so open each candle’s own page before you buy, especially if the person you’re buying for is particular about fragrance. Our profile of The Vintage Hamper Company has a little more.

If you’d rather buy in person: The Duchess of Candles

The maker that got this site started sells at craft events rather than online. The Duchess of Candles pours coloured soy into vintage cups, small teapots, milk jugs and china shoes, with the wax tinted to pick up a colour from the china. Their Instagram is the place to find out where they’ll be next.

Our budget pick: pour one yourself

The cheapest good teacup candle gift is one you make. A cup from a charity shop, a bag of container soy and a wick are all you need, and a handmade candle in a cup you chose for someone means a lot. Our piece on why a handmade present means more goes into why.

Supplies For Candles’ Starter Candle Kit is one place to start, from a UK candle supplier. Our round-up of the best candle making kits explains what a kit needs for teacups, and our step-by-step instructable takes you through your first one. Give yourself a week or two, as scented soy smells better once it has cured.

Gift sets of two or more cups

Boxed sets of two, three or four teacup candles suit households, mums and grandmas, and dinner tables where more than one candle gets lit. Most handmade makers sell single cups, so sets are easiest to compare on eBay, where small makers and gift shops both list them.

Making it personal

Tuck a note in the box saying why you chose that cup or that scent. A care card is worth adding too: trim the wick to 5mm, burn it for two to three hours the first time, never more than four hours at once, and stop when there’s 1cm of wax left. Our safety and care guide has the full list.
