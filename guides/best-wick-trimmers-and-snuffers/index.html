Most candles that smoke, flicker or leave black marks on the cup have one thing in common: a wick that’s too long. A wick trimmer fixes that in a couple of seconds. A snuffer is less essential, but it puts a candle out neatly, without blowing smoke round the room or splashing wax. Together they’re the two tools we’d suggest to anyone who burns candles regularly, and they suit teacup candles especially well.

How we chose

We haven’t tried every trimmer and snuffer on sale, and most are very similar, so we’ve picked by type. We looked at how well each one works on a candle in a cup, where the wick sits below the rim and gets lower as the candle burns, and at how sturdy and easy to clean it is. What to look for.

  • A trimmer with an angled head and long handles, so it reaches down into a cup and cuts the wick flat.
  • A trimmer with a flat foot on the blade that catches the trimmed end, so it doesn’t fall into the wax.
  • A snuffer with a long handle and a bell small enough to fit inside a teacup.
  • Stainless steel cleans most easily. Plated finishes look lovely but can wear over time.

Why it’s our pick. If you only buy one tool, make it this one. Kitchen scissors are fine for the first burn, but once the wax has gone down a centimetre or two they can’t reach, and they cut at an angle. A trimmer cuts cleanly at any depth.

Good alternatives

A wick dipper. A thin metal hook used to push the lit wick down into the melted wax and then lift it straight again. It puts the flame out with no smoke at all and coats the wick in wax ready for next time. It’s a nice tool to have, but you need to straighten the wick straight away or it sets bent.

A bell snuffer on its own. If you mainly want to put candles out without smoke, a snuffer is enough. Choose a small bell for teacups. Large church-style snuffers are made for tall candles and are awkward in a cup.

How to use them

Trimming. Trim the wick to about 5mm before each burn, with the candle cool and the wax set. Pick up any trimmed bits and charred ends so they don’t end up in the wax, where they can catch light.

Snuffing. Lower the bell gently over the flame and hold it for a few seconds until the flame is out. Lift it straight up. Don’t press the bell into the wax.

Dipping. With the wax fully melted, push the wick sideways into the pool until the flame goes out, then lift it straight up again with the hook and leave it centred.

Blowing a candle out works, of course, but it can spray hot wax onto the cup and the table and leaves a trail of smoke. A teacup’s wide, shallow shape makes splashes more likely than a deep jar.

Keeping them clean

Wipe the trimmer and snuffer with kitchen paper while they’re still warm to lift off soot and wax. For stubborn marks, a little washing-up liquid and warm water does the job. Dry them properly so steel doesn’t spot.
