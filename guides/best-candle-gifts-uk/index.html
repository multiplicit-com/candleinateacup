Candles are one of the most popular presents in the UK, which is exactly why it’s worth putting a bit of thought into the one you give. Plenty of people already have a cupboard with a couple of unlit jar candles in it. A candle that’s been made by hand, chosen for the person or that does something a bit different stands out from the pile.

How we chose

For the handmade candles, we picked real candles from UK makers whose listings describe the cup, the wax and the scent properly, and we link straight to them. We checked those links on 23 November 2026 but haven’t bought from the makers ourselves. For the cheaper, more everyday gifts, such as wax melts and care sets, the models on sale change every few weeks, so we describe what to look for and the buttons open current Amazon and eBay listings of that kind.

Choosing a scent for someone else

Scent is personal, and a candle that smells wonderful to you might be too much for someone else. A few things help:

  • Go gentle and familiar. Fresh linen, tea, vanilla, rose and light florals are easy to like. Very sweet, very smoky or very strong scents are harder to get right for another person.
  • Think about where it’ll go. A kitchen candle wants something fresh. A bedroom or living room suits softer, warmer scents.
  • Unscented is a real option. For people who don’t get on with fragrance, or for a dining table, an unscented candle gives the light and the look on its own.
  • Ask the maker. Small makers are usually happy to suggest something if you tell them a bit about the person.

Wrapping and a care card

A plain box, tissue and a handwritten tag suit a handmade candle better than glossy paper. Add a small card with care instructions: trim the wick to 5mm, burn it for two to three hours the first time, never more than four hours at once, and never leave it unattended.
