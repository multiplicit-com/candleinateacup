Easter is the first proper get-together of the year for a lot of families, and a teacup candle is a nice thing to take with you. It looks cheerful on a spring table, it’s a change from another chocolate egg, and the cup is still there to keep once the wax has gone. This guide covers when Easter falls, the scents and colours that suit it, where to buy a candle and how to make your own in time.

When is Easter?

Easter moves every year, which is why it can creep up on you. In 2027, Easter Sunday is on 28 March, with Good Friday on 26 March and Easter Monday on 29 March. Mothering Sunday is three weeks earlier, on 7 March, so if you’re buying for both, it’s worth ordering at the same time. Our Mother’s Day teacup candles guide covers that one.

Spring scents

By late March most people are ready for something lighter than the spice and woodsmoke of winter candles. These scents tend to suit Easter:

  • Fresh florals: hyacinth, sweet pea, lily of the valley and narcissus, which smell like spring bulbs coming up.
  • Citrus: lemon, grapefruit and lemon with a little lavender, bright without being heavy.
  • Clean scents: cotton, linen and fresh-cut grass for someone who doesn’t like anything sweet.
  • Bakery scents: hot cross bun, vanilla and chocolate, kept light, if you’d rather it smelled like Easter baking.

If the candle is for the table at Easter lunch, a light scent or none at all is best, so it doesn’t fight with the food. Our guide to scented teacup candles explains how scent works in a candle.

Pastel colours and spring cups

Pale yellow, lilac, mint green, duck egg blue and soft pink are the Easter colours most people recognise. Natural ivory soy also looks lovely in a cup with a busy pattern. Our colour palettes guide has a spring set if you’re making your own.

The cup is what makes it feel like Easter. Look out for:

  • Spring flowers on the china. Daffodils, primroses, violets and blossom all turn up on vintage cups.
  • Yellow and green patterns. These look fresh and cheerful even without flowers.
  • Pale lustre or pastel cups. Some mid-century china came in soft pastel glazes that suit Easter wax colours perfectly.
  • A cup with a saucer. It catches drips and looks finished on a table. Our guide to teacup candle saucers covers why.

A few odd cups in pastel colours, each with a candle, make a pretty centrepiece down the middle of an Easter table. Leave enough space around each one, and keep them clear of napkins, paper decorations and dried flowers. Our safety and care guide has more on that.

Where to buy one

Small UK makers are the best place for a one-off cup and a hand-poured candle. The Vintage Hamper Company’s vintage china candles are worth a look if you’d like to compare several cups at once, and The Lime Garden’s Duchess Vintage Tea Cup Candle on Folksy was a floral bone china cup with a choice of scents, including sweet pea and lavender, when we checked on 23 November 2026. Whichever you choose, check the wax, the scent and whether a saucer comes with it. Our teacup candle gift guide has the full list of things to check, and our teacup candle gifts by price guide shows what you tend to get at each budget.

Order early

Most teacup candles are poured by hand in small batches, and Easter comes soon after Mothering Sunday, which is a busy time for candle makers. Ordering in early March gives you a better choice of cups and time for the post, especially as Good Friday and Easter Monday are bank holidays.

Making your own

A candle you’ve poured yourself, in a cup you picked for someone, is a lovely thing to give when you visit. It’s also a good Easter holiday project if you’ve got a free afternoon. The wax needs a day to set, and a scented soy candle smells much stronger once it has cured for one to two weeks. For Easter Sunday, aim to pour by mid-March. If you’re making candles for Mothering Sunday too, pour both batches at the same time.

A few ideas that work well:

  • A set of pastel cups. Three odd cups, each with a different pale colour of wax, poured from one batch of wax.
  • A cup with a daffodil on it. Pair it with a fresh, green scent or leave it unscented.
  • A decorated cup. Paint a plain white cup with a few spring flowers or someone’s name. See decorating china yourself.
  • A refill for a cup they already have. If someone has an old teacup candle that has burned down, our guide to refilling teacup candles shows how to fill it again as a present.

Wrapping and a care card

A small box lined with tissue or shredded paper keeps the cup safe, and a ribbon in a spring colour makes it feel like an Easter present. Wrap the saucer separately and put it underneath.

Add a small card with how to look after it: trim the wick to 5mm before lighting, let it burn for two to three hours the first time so the melted wax reaches the edge, never burn it for more than four hours at once, and stop when there’s about 1cm of wax left.
