Handmade candles versus the big-brand jar: why a maker's candle makes the better present

By Steven · Updated 23 November 2026

Let’s start by being fair. The big-brand jar candle, with Yankee Candle the best known of them, is a perfectly good thing. You know what you’re getting, you can buy one in most towns, and the scent names alone have kept people amused for years. Plenty of us have a half-burned jar of something called a cosy winter feeling at the back of a cupboard, and we’re not here to make anyone feel bad about it.

But if you’re buying a candle as a present, we’d make a gentle case for the handmade one. Here’s why, in no particular order and with tongue only partly in cheek.

Nobody else will have the same one

A big-brand candle is, by design, the same as millions of others. That’s the point of it, and it’s why it’s reliable. It also means that if you give one to an aunt at Christmas, there’s a fair chance she already has it, or has been given it before, or will be given it again by someone else on Boxing Day.

A teacup candle from a small maker is very often a one-off. The cup might be a 1950s pattern that hasn’t been made for decades, picked out at a car boot sale on a cold Sunday morning. Nobody else on the street will have that cup with that colour of wax in it. When it’s burned down, she still has a pretty vintage cup and saucer, which is more than you can say for an empty glass jar with a sticky label on it.

Someone actually chose it

Behind most handmade candles is one person who chose the cup, decided the wax should be a soft pink to pick up the roses on the rim, and matched the scent to suit. That sort of care shows, and people notice it when they unwrap it. A maker at a craft fair will often tell you where the cup came from and why they went for that scent, and you can pass the story on with the present. It’s hard to say the same for a candle chosen by a team in an office a long way away.

You can ask what’s in it

Small makers are usually happy to tell you exactly what’s in their candles: which wax, which wick, how much fragrance and how long to burn it the first time. Many will make one without scent for someone with allergies, or change the colour to match a room. Try asking for that at a shopping centre till.

It can come back for a refill

Some makers will refill their own cups when the wax runs out, so the present can carry on for years. If yours doesn’t, our guide to refilling a teacup candle shows how to do it at home, even if you’ve never made a candle.

The money goes somewhere nice

When you buy from someone selling at a craft fair, a Christmas market or their own online shop, the money goes to a person, often someone doing it around a day job or school runs because they love it. It might pay for their next pitch fee, more wax or a box of cups. There’s a good feeling in that, and it’s one that a big-brand receipt doesn’t come with.

In fairness to the big jar

To be even-handed, there are times the big-brand candle wins:

  • You need a present in the next hour. The big brands are everywhere, and that’s hard to beat on Christmas Eve.
  • You know exactly which scent someone loves. If your mum has burned the same scent for twenty years, she probably wants another one, and who are we to argue.
  • You want something that will look the same every time. Handmade candles vary from one to the next, which is part of the charm, but not if you’re buying six to match.
  • There’s a sale on. When we popped into a shop recently, big jars were marked down from £24.99 to around half that. Prices like that come and go, but if you’re happy with a big-brand jar, it’s worth keeping an eye out.

How to find a good maker

The best place to start is a craft fair or Christmas market, where you can see and smell the candles and chat to the person who made them. Online, look for makers who show clear photos of the actual cup you’ll get, say what wax they use, include burning instructions and pack carefully for the post. Our makers section has a growing list of independent teacup candle makers in the UK, and our gift guide explains what to look for in a good one.

If you’d rather make one yourself, that’s the most personal present of all. Our step-by-step instructable and our round-up of candle making kits will get you started, and our Christmas teacup candle gifts guide has festive scent and colour ideas.

The short version

A big-brand jar candle is a safe present. A handmade teacup candle is a thoughtful one, with a story, a one-off cup and a real person behind it. If you’ve got the time to choose, we know which we’d rather unwrap.
