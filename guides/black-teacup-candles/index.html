Black teacup candles

By Steven · Updated 22 November 2026

  • Black and gold cups suit black wax best. Pale floral china can look odd with it.
  • Most black teacup candles are handmade, so eBay and small makers have the widest choice.
  • Black soy wax often looks deep charcoal or plum rather than jet black, which is normal.
  • Smoky, dark fruit and spiced scents go well with black wax.

Most teacup candles are pastel wax in flowery china, so a black one stands out on a craft fair table or a shelf. They’re popular with people who like gothic and dark interiors, they make an obvious Halloween gift, and they look good on a dinner table in winter when everything else is candlelight and dark wood.

Which cups suit black wax

The cup matters more with black wax than with any other colour. Black wax in a pale floral cup can look a bit heavy, as if the cup has been filled with tar. It works much better in dark or strongly patterned china.

Black and gold coffee cups. Small black cups with a gold rim, a gold Greek key border and a band of classical figures are common in second-hand shops and on eBay. Many were made in Greece as souvenirs, and the bases are often marked with the amount of gold used in the decoration. They’re a natural match for black wax, and the gold catches the candlelight.

Deep jewel colours. Cobalt blue, burgundy, bottle green and dark purple china all sit well with black wax, especially with gilding.

Black and white transferware. Toile-style prints, silhouettes and monochrome patterns make a black candle look deliberate rather than gloomy.

Gothic novelty cups. New cups with skulls, bats, ravens or spider webs are easy to find. They’re not vintage, but they suit a Halloween gift.

What colour to expect

Black candles, and black soy candles in particular, are rarely jet black. Soy wax sets opaque and slightly pale, so even a lot of dye tends to give a very dark charcoal, and sometimes the wax looks plum or navy in certain light. The candle in the photo above is a good example. In the cup it reads as black, but the wax is actually a deep grey with a hint of purple. Paraffin and some blended waxes take colour more deeply and look blacker.

Soy wax also tends to develop a white bloom on the surface, called frosting. It’s harmless, but it’s much more noticeable on black wax than on pastels. A few white streaks on a handmade black soy candle are normal and not a sign of a fault.

Scents that suit black candles

A black candle that smells of candyfloss can feel a bit odd. Scents that tend to match the look include:

  • smoky woods, incense, oud and leather
  • dark fruit such as black cherry, blackberry and plum
  • warm spice, clove, black pepper and tobacco
  • for Halloween, pumpkin spice and toffee apple

Unscented black candles are worth considering too, especially for a dinner table.

As a gift

Black teacup candles are a good present for anyone whose taste runs to dark florals, Victorian or gothic decor, and for Halloween parties, where a few black cups on a table look better than plastic decorations. They also make a good choice for someone who likes the idea of a teacup candle but would never have pink in the house.

If you’re buying a vintage one, check the gold on the rim and handle in the photos. Worn gilding is common on old black and gold cups and doesn’t affect the candle, but it does change how the cup looks once it’s empty.
