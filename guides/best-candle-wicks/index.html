The wick decides more about how a candle burns than anything else, including the wax. Too small and the candle tunnels down the middle, leaving a thick ring of wax on the sides. Too big and it smokes, flickers and gets the cup hotter than it should. Teacups add their own twist, because they’re wider and shallower than most candle jars. This guide covers what to buy and how to work out which size you need.

How we chose

We haven’t tested every wick on sale, so we’ve picked by type rather than by brand. We looked at what works well in round china cups: wicks that are easy to fix and centre, that come in a useful range of sizes and that suppliers give sizing charts for. The links take you to current listings so you can compare packs and suppliers.

Wick series and sizes

Cotton wicks come in series, with names such as ECO, CD, HTP and LX. Each series is braided a little differently, and each comes in a range of sizes, usually with a number such as ECO 4 or CD 10. A bigger number gives a bigger flame. Any of the common series can work in a teacup. What matters most is picking the right size within whichever series you use, and sticking with that series while you learn.

What to check.

  • That the pack names the series and sizes. A pack that just says “small, medium, large” is hard to repeat.
  • The length. It should be longer than the depth of your cup.
  • That the sizes suit container candles. Most pre-tabbed packs do.

Both Candle Shack and Supplies For Candles sell wooden wicks with the metal clips they need.

Wick stickers and centring bars. Not wicks, but nearly as important. A sticker holds the tab to the bottom of the cup, and a centring bar or a clothes peg keeps the wick upright while the wax sets. A wick that sets off-centre burns unevenly and heats one side of the cup.

Coloured wicks. Black and coloured cotton wicks look striking in a dark or bright candle. See the section on coloured wicks for more.

Two smaller wicks. For cups wider than about 9cm, one wick may not melt all the way to the edge. Our guide to multi-wick candles explains how to size and place two or three.

What to avoid

  • Untreated string or garden twine. It burns too fast and too hot, and smokes.
  • Wicks with no series or size given. You can’t repeat a good result if you don’t know what you used.
  • Choosing by flame size in the first ten minutes. Wicks settle down as the melt pool forms. Judge them after two to three hours.

Wick length and trimming

Choose wicks a few centimetres longer than the depth of your cup, so there’s enough to hold in a centring bar while the wax sets. Trim to about 5mm before the first burn and before every burn after that. A long wick smokes and burns hotter than it should.
