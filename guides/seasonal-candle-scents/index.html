Seasonal candle scents: a guide to the year, from Christmas to Bonfire Night

Scent is the easiest way to make a candle feel right for the time of year. Orange and clove feels right in December, cut grass and lilac in April and toffee apple on Bonfire Night. This guide runs through the year season by season, with the scents that sell and burn well, the colours and cups that go with them, and a few teacup touches. We’ll be going into each season in more depth nearer the time.

If you’re making your own, our guide to scenting teacup candles covers fragrance oils, how much to add and how long to cure them.

Christmas

Christmas is the busiest time of year for candles, and the scents are the most familiar:

  • Spice: cinnamon, clove, nutmeg and gingerbread.
  • Fruit: orange, clementine and cranberry, often with spice.
  • Forest: pine, fir, cedar and the smell of a real Christmas tree.
  • Food and drink: mulled wine, Christmas pudding, mince pies and brandy butter.
  • Church: frankincense and myrrh.

Orange and cinnamon is a safe bet for nearly anyone. Deep red, forest green or gold wax looks festive, and a cup with holly, gold rims or a winter scene finishes it off. Our Christmas teacup candle gifts guide has more ideas for giving.

Winter after Christmas

Once the decorations come down, the heavy spice can feel like too much. January and February suit softer, comforting scents:

  • Vanilla, tonka bean and amber.
  • Cashmere, fresh cotton and clean linen.
  • Hot chocolate, toasted marshmallow and salted caramel.
  • Chai and spiced black tea, a gentler spice than Christmas.

Cream, oatmeal and soft grey wax suit these well, as do plain white or blue-and-white cups. Vanilla scents can turn wax yellow or brown over time, so they’re best in cream or caramel wax.

Valentine’s Day

Rose is the classic, but there’s plenty more:

  • Rose, peony and red berries.
  • Champagne, strawberry and pink grapefruit.
  • Dark chocolate, black cherry and vanilla.

Pink and red wax in a cup with roses on it is hard to beat, and a pair of cups makes a nice present for two.

Mother’s Day

Mothering Sunday in the UK falls three weeks before Easter, so it moves around between March and early April. Mother’s Day scents are usually soft and floral:

  • Peony, sweet pea and lily of the valley.
  • Rose and Earl Grey, which feels right in a pretty cup.
  • Lavender with white tea, or pear and freesia for something light.

Pastel wax and a cup with flowers on it is the classic look. A vintage cup can also be a way of giving something with a bit of history, especially if it’s a pattern she remembers from her own mother’s or grandmother’s house.

Easter and spring

Spring scents are fresh and green after the heavier winter ones:

  • Flowers: lilac, hyacinth, freesia, narcissus and blossom.
  • Fresh and green: cut grass, green tea, cucumber and mint.
  • Citrus: lemon, lime and bergamot.
  • Easter baking: hot cross buns, lemon drizzle and simnel cake.

Pale yellow, mint green and lilac wax in cups with spring flowers looks lovely. Jasmine tea and green tea scents fit the teacup theme nicely at this time of year.

Summer

Summer candles tend to be light and fruity, and many people burn them less, so a smaller cup can be a better buy:

  • Garden flowers: sweet pea, honeysuckle, rose and jasmine.
  • Fruit: strawberries and cream, raspberry, peach and rhubarb.
  • Fresh: elderflower, cucumber, lemonade and sea salt.
  • Afternoon tea in the garden: Victoria sponge, scones and jam, and iced tea.

Soft pink, peach and pale blue wax work well, and summery chintz cups are ideal. Keep candles out of direct sun on a windowsill in summer, as warm wax can soften and colours can fade.

Autumn

As the evenings draw in, scents get richer and warmer again:

  • Orchard fruit: apple, pear, fig and blackberry.
  • Woods: cedar, sandalwood, oak moss and fallen leaves.
  • Warm: cinnamon apple, honey and oat, and maple.
  • Tea: chai, rooibos and spiced black tea.

Ochre, burnt orange, deep red and brown wax suit autumn, especially in cups with fruit or leaf patterns.

Halloween and Bonfire Night

The end of October and the start of November have their own scents, many of them from the bonfire and the fair:

  • Pumpkin spice, toffee apple and caramel.
  • Treacle toffee and parkin, the gingerbread made for Bonfire Night in the north of England.
  • Smoky woods, bonfire and black pepper.
  • Black cherry, plum and dark berries.

Halloween is the time for black, deep purple and orange wax. Our guides to black teacup candles and making black candle wax show how to get a good, deep black.

Tea scents for any time of year

Because these are teacup candles, tea scents always make sense, and there’s one for each season:

  • Spring: jasmine tea and green tea.
  • Summer: iced tea, mint tea and lemon.
  • Autumn: chai and rooibos.
  • Winter: Earl Grey, spiced black tea and English breakfast with honey.

Tips for seasonal candles

Make or order early. Soy candles need a week or two to cure before the scent is at its best, so start Christmas candles in October or early November. For buying, makers get busy well before the big days.

Go easy on spice. Cinnamon, clove and some other spice fragrances are strong and can be irritating for some people in a small room. Stay within your fragrance supplier’s recommended amount and check its safety data sheet, which also tells you what has to go on the label if you sell the candles.

Think about who it’s for. For someone with allergies or asthma, or if you’re not sure what they like, an unscented candle in a seasonal cup is a kinder present.

Match the cup to the season. Holly in December, primroses in spring, fruit in autumn. Our colour palettes guide has ideas for matching wax to the pattern on the cup.
