Burning and safety

How long does a teacup candle burn?

It depends on how much wax is in the cup, the type of wax and the wick. As a rough guide, an average teacup holding 150g to 200g of soy wax will usually give somewhere in the region of 25 to 40 hours, spread over many evenings. Burn it for no more than four hours at a time. Our burn time calculator gives a rough figure for your own cup, and the safety and care guide has more.

Are teacup candles safe?

Yes, if the cup is sound and you follow the usual candle rules. Check the cup for cracks first, trim the wick to about 5mm, stand it on its saucer away from anything that can catch fire, never leave it burning in an empty room, and stop when about 1cm of wax is left. The safety guide covers it all.

Why has my candle burned a hole down the middle?

That's called tunnelling. Either the wick is too small for the cup, or the first burn was too short. On the first burn, let the melted wax reach the edge of the cup, which usually takes two to three hours. See the safety and care guide.

Can I just put a tealight in a teacup?

Yes. Put a layer of sand in the bottom, sit one tealight on top and stand the cup on its saucer. Keep tealights apart from each other. See teacup tealight holders.

The cup

Can I use a cracked or chipped cup?

Not for a real candle. Heat can open up a crack, and the cup can split. Small chips on the rim are usually only cosmetic, but a chip with a crack running from it is not. Use damaged cups with a battery tealight instead. Our teacup preparation guide shows how to check a cup.

Is a crazed cup OK to use?

Usually, yes. Crazing is in the glaze, not the body of the cup, and many people like the antique look it gives. Check that the cup still rings when you tap it and that the glaze isn't flaking. See caring for old china for more on crazing, including lightening stained crazing.

Can a teacup candle cup go in the dishwasher?

We'd keep old china out of the dishwasher, before and after it's a candle. Dishwashers wear away gold rims and can dull lustre and painted decoration. Wax can also clog the dishwasher. Wash it by hand in warm, soapy water.

Can I use a cup with a gold rim?

Yes. Keep the wax at least 1cm below the gold, don't scrub the gold and keep the cup out of the dishwasher and microwave. See teacup preparation.

What else can I make a candle in?

Stoneware mugs, sugar bowls, sturdy glass jars and metal tins all work well. Avoid thin glass, plastic, wood and anything cracked. See candles beyond china.

Making your own

What is the best wax for teacup candles?

Container soy wax is the most popular choice. It's easy to use, sticks well to china and burns slowly. Rapeseed and coconut blends also work very well, and paraffin gives stronger colours and scent. Our guide to wax types compares them.

What size wick do I need for a teacup?

Measure the inside width of the cup at the height the wax will reach, then check your wick supplier's chart for your type of wax. Do a test burn to be sure. Cups wider than about 9cm may need two smaller wicks. See the wax and wicks guide.

How much wax do I need for a teacup?

Fill the cup with water to about 1cm below the rim, weigh the water in grams and multiply by 0.86. That's roughly how much soy wax you need. Step 2 of our instructable explains it.

Why has white frosting appeared on my soy candle?

Frosting is a white, crystalline bloom that natural soy wax often develops. It's harmless and doesn't change how the candle burns. Pouring a little cooler and letting the candle cool slowly helps. More in how to make teacup candles.

Can I colour candle wax with crayons?

It's best not to. Crayons are coloured with pigment that doesn't dissolve in wax, and the particles clog the wick. Use dye made for candles. Our colouring guide explains which dyes work.

Do I need to let a teacup candle cure before burning it?

An unscented candle can be burned once it has fully set, after at least 24 hours. A scented one smells stronger if you let it cure for one to two weeks first. See the instructable.

Refilling and reusing

Can I drink out of a teacup that used to be a candle?

We wouldn't. Candle wax, fragrance oil and dye aren't made to be eaten or drunk, and a thin film of them tends to stay on the china even after washing. Old cups often have crazing in the glaze, and those fine cracks can hold on to wax and fragrance in a way you can't scrub out. Some older china also has lead in the glaze or the decoration, which was common before today's limits came in, and a cup that has been heated by a candle for hours may have small cracks you can't see.

Once a cup has been a candle, it's best kept for decoration: another candle, a battery tealight, a plant or your pens. If you really want to use one for drinks again, only consider a cup with sound, uncrazed glaze and no decoration inside or on the rim, clean it very thoroughly as described in caring for old china, and accept that you're taking a small risk.

Can I refill a teacup candle when it runs out?

Yes. Get the last of the wax out by freezing the cup or floating it out with hot tap water, remove the old wick tab, wash the cup and make a new candle in it with a fresh wick. Caring for old china explains how to get the wax out, and our instructable covers making a new candle.

Can I make a teacup candle from old candle stubs?

Yes, as long as you sort the wax by type and use a new wick. Melt the wax gently and strain it to remove soot and old wick. See melting old candles.
